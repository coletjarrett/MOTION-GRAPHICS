What does production like this actually cost?
[pause 0.3]
Take the film on Daniel chapter two:
three minutes, fully animated, narrated, and scored with sound design.
The person directing it sent fewer than ten short messages.
The AI assistant planned the scenes, wrote every line of animation code,
built the sound, and checked the frames for defects.
The final render ran on a laptop, in the background, in about twenty minutes.
No render farm. No stock footage. No licensed plugins.

This graphics library went further.
Dozens of clips, in more than a dozen visual styles,
were designed, rendered, and reviewed overnight.
Several AI workers built style packs in parallel,
while a second model critiqued the results.
Each lower third renders in well under a minute.

Traditionally, a package like this could take a motion designer several weeks.
Here, the human role shifts from building to directing:
choosing, approving, and refining.
The running costs are a few AI subscriptions,
and the electricity for one laptop.
That frees skilled volunteers to focus on what matters most:
the message.
